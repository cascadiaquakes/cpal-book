# Data Compilations

The paleoseismology records displayed in the CRESCENT CPAL Viewer are drawn
from the USGS Powell Center Cascadia earthquake hazards working group's
published compilation of onshore and offshore paleoseismic data along the
Cascadia Subduction Zone.

## Primary source

**USGS ScienceBase catalog entry:**  
<a href="https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/64261ec8d34e370832ff6232" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Compiled onshore and offshore paleoseismic data along the Cascadia Subduction zone</a>

The USGS entry is the authoritative source for the underlying data,
methodology, age-ranking scheme, evidence-ranking scheme, and full field
metadata. Users looking for details about how a specific record was
evaluated or the sedimentological / analytical basis for a given entry
should consult the ScienceBase page directly.

## About the compilation

The Powell Center working group compiled published onshore and offshore
paleoseismic data along the Cascadia subduction zone, spanning sites from
Vancouver Island to the Mendocino Triple Junction. Evidence for megathrust
rupture includes:

- Coastal land-level change
- Tsunami inundation
- Onshore shaking proxies (e.g. landslides, liquefaction)
- Offshore shaking proxies (e.g. marine turbidites)

The quality of paleoseismic data for megathrust rupture varies because
analytical capabilities and data collection methodologies have evolved
over the past three decades. The compilation therefore includes two
ranking schemes:

- **Age ranking** — "How well is a proposed paleoseismic event dated?"
  based on the materials and methods used.
- **Evidence ranking** — "How confident are we that a proposed event is,
  in fact, the result of a Cascadia megathrust rupture?" based on
  sedimentological characteristics, correlation, and mapping. The
  evidence ranking also helps evaluate alternative mechanisms such as
  crustal fault, intraslab, or distant tsunamigenic earthquakes.

## In the CPAL Viewer

The CPAL Viewer surfaces this compilation as four record-type layers
(coastal deformation, marine shaking, terrestrial shaking, tsunami),
each downloadable in a condensed GeoJSON form. For the full compilation
metadata, age/evidence ranking definitions, and the underlying source
publications, always refer to the USGS ScienceBase entry linked above.
